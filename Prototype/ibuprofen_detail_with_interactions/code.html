Drug Reference

Ibuprofen

Propionic acid derivative - Non-selective COX-1 & COX-2 inhibitor - First marketed 1969 (Brufen, UK)

OTC + Rx

STANDARD ADULT DOSE (OTC)

200–400 mg every 4–6 hrs

Max: 1,200 mg/day

STANDARD ADULT DOSE (RX)

400–800 mg every 6–8 hrs

Max: 3,200 mg/day

ONSET / HALF-LIFE

30–60 min onset

~2 hours half-life

CLINICAL INDICATIONS

OA, RA, fever, dysmenorrhea, headache, dental pain, mild-moderate pain, migraine

CLINICAL INTERACTIONS — DRUG-DRUG INTERACTION MATRIX

Drug Class / Agent Interaction Effect & Mechanism Severity
Anticoagulants (e.g., Warfarin, Rivaroxaban) Synergistic inhibition of platelet aggregation and gastric mucosal damage. Significantly increased risk of GI bleeding and hemorrhage. Major
SSRI Antidepressants (e.g., Sertraline, Fluoxetine) Increased risk of upper GI bleeding due to serotonin's role in platelet activation combined with NSAID-induced gastric irritation. Moderate
Glucocorticoids (e.g., Prednisone) Marked increase in the risk of gastrointestinal ulceration and perforation when co-administered with NSAIDs. Major
ACE Inhibitors / ARBs (e.g., Lisinopril) Reduction in antihypertensive effect and increased risk of renal impairment, especially in elderly or dehydrated patients. Moderate
Diuretics (e.g., Furosemide) NSAIDs can reduce the natriuretic effect of diuretics in some patients due to inhibition of renal prostaglandin synthesis. Minor
warning

Max OTC duration: 10 days for pain, 3 days for fever. Take with food. At high doses, CV risk increases. Not recommended in third trimester of pregnancy.
