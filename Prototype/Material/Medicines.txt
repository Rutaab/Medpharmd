1. Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS)
ORS is the mainstay treatment for dehydration, especially caused by acute diarrhea or vomiting. The WHO-approved
formula uses a balanced mix of electrolytes and glucose to replace fluid and electrolyte losses.
e Mechanism of Action: The glucose in ORS facilitates the absorption of sodium and water in the small
intestine via the sodium-glucose co-transport system, thereby correcting dehydration and acidosis.
e Main Indications:
o Dehydration from acute diarrhea in adults and children {all ages)
o Mild to moderate dehydration from vomiting or other fluid losses
o Maintenance therapy to prevent dehydration in ongoing diarrhea
o Asan adjunct in febrile ilinesses to prevent dehydration
o In hyperemesis gravidarum (pregnancy)
e Available Formulations: Powder for reconstitution (most common), ready-to-drink liquid, and rice-based
ORS (for persistent diarrhea).

# Antiepileptic Drugs: An Overview
Antiepileptic drugs (AEDs), also known as anticonvulsants or antiseizure medications (ASMs), are a diverse group of
medications used primarily to prevent and control seizures in individuals with epilepsy. Their goal is to stabilize the
electrical activity of the central nervous system by targeting specific receptors and ion channels.

e Sodium Channel Blockers: These drugs (e.g., phenytoin, carbamazepine, lamotrigine) stabilize the
inactivated state of voltage-gated sodium channels, reducing the ability of neurons to fire at high
frequencies, thereby limiting the spread of seizure activity.

٠ GABA Enhancers: Gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) is the primary inhibitory neurotransmitter in the brain.
Drugs like phenobarbital (a barbiturate) and benzodiazepines (e.g., clonazepam) enhance the action of
GABA, leading to neuronal inhibition.

e Multiple Mechanisms: Several AEDs have complex or multiple mechanisms, which contribute to their broad-
spectrum efficacy. For example, valproate enhances GABA activity, blocks sodium channels, and may also
modulate glutamate activity.

٠ Rapid Rise in Use: Levetiracetam is a newer AED that binds to synaptic vesicle protein 2A (SV2A), modulating
neurotransmitter release. It is particularly noted for its favorable side effect profile and is often used as a
first-line agent for various seizure types.

٠ Additional Benefits: Beyond seizure control, some AEDs have other uses. For instance, carbamazepine is
effective for neuropathic pain, and valproate is used for migraine prophylaxis and bipolar disorder, while
lamotrigine is effective for mood stabilization in bipolar disorder.

drug interactions. A study in Karachi found Levetiracetam (38%) was the most prescribed AED, though valproic
acid remained common in children.

increase in heart rate and strength of contraction (beta-1).

# Macrolides: Class Overview

Macrolides are a class of antibiotics that work by binding to the bacterial ribosome, effectively stopping protein
synthesis. They are a key option for treating a wide range of common infections, particularly when a patient is
allergic to penicillin.

e Common Mechanism: They inhibit bacterial protein synthesis by binding to the 50S ribosomal subunit. This
action is generally bacteriostatic, but can be bactericidal against certain bacteria depending on the drug
concentration and bacterial species.

٠ Main Indications: Their versatile spectrum makes them valuable for treating:

o Respiratory Tract infections: Including pneumonia, bronchitis, sinusitis, and pharyngitis (often a
go-to alternative for penicillin-allergic patients).

o Skin and Soft Tissue Infections: Such as cellulitis and impetigo.

o Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs): For example, uncomplicated genital chlamydia and non-
gonococcal urethritis.

telithromycin. Patients should be monitored for signs of jaundice or liver injury.

O Summary of General Precautions for Aminoglycosides

--- Page 86 ---
٠ Avoid Concurrent Nephrotoxic/Ototoxic Drugs: The risk of toxicity is significantly increased when used with
loop diuretics (e.g., furosemide), vancomycin, other aminoglycosides, and certain chemotherapeutic agents.

e Monitor Renal Function: Check serum creatinine and calculate creatinine clearance at baseline and
regularly (e.g., every 2-3 days) during therapy. Adjust dose and/or interval based on results.

٠ Monitor for Ototoxicity: Instruct patients to report tinnitus, hearing loss, dizziness, or vertigo. Objective
audiometric testing may be warranted for prolonged courses.

e Neuromuscular Blockade: Aminoglycosides can cause neuromuscular blockade, which may be hazardous in
patients with myasthenia gravis, Parkinson's disease, or those receiving neuromuscular blocking agents.

e Avoid Prolonged Use: Use for the shortest duration necessary to treat the infection (e.g., 7-10 days) to
minimize toxicity.

e Pregnancy: Generally classified as Pregnancy Category D (positive evidence of human fetal risk).
Aminoglycosides can cause irreversible congenital deafness in the infant if used during pregnancy. Use only
when absolutely necessary and benefit outweighs risk.

O ANote on Fluoroquinolone Safety
Fluoroquinolones are potent antibiotics with a risk of serious adverse effects, including tendinitis/tendon rupture,
peripheral neuropathy, and CNS effects. Their use is generally restricted and not recommended for uncomplicated
infections when safer alternatives exist. Due to the risk of arthropathy, their use in children and adolescents is
restricted and is only considered when the benefits clearly outweigh the risks. Always adhere to the following general
precautions:

‎e Drug Interactions: Divalent/trivalent cations (e.g., aluminum/magnesium antacids, iron, zinc, calcium
supplements, dairy products) can bind to these drugs and prevent their absorption. Oral doses should be
taken at least 2 hours before or 2-4 hours after these products.

‎e Monitoring: Patients should be monitored for signs of tendon pain, neuropathy, or CNS effects (e.g.,
dizziness, confusion). The medication should be discontinued immediately if such symptoms occur.

‎e Prescribing: Fiuoroquinofones should be prescribed with caution in the elderly, those with renal impairment
(dose adjustment required), and patients taking corticosteroids.

O Important Note on Cephalosporin Use and Safety

Cephalosporins are broad-spectrum, bactericidal antibiotics that inhibit bacterial cell wall synthesis. Key points to
remember:

٠ Cross-allergy: Approximately 5-1096 of penicillin-allergic patients may also be allergic to cephalosporins.
Caution is advised.

e Renal Impairment: Ail cephalosporins (especially cefoperazone and ceftazidime) require dose adjustment in
renal failure; accumulation can lead to neurotoxicity (seizures).

٠ Pediatric Use: Generally safe in children, but dosage must be calculated based on body weight. Certain
formulations are not suitable for neonates or infants under 1 month of age.

٠ Clostridium difficile-associated diarrhea (CDAD): Broad-spectrum cephalosporins are a common cause of
CDAD; monitor for persistent diarrhea.

e Neuromuscular excitability: High doses, especially in renal failure, can provoke seizures. This is more
common with cefepime and ceftazidime.

e Pregnancy: Generally considered safe (FDA Category B), except for cefoperazone (a 3rd-gen cephalosporin),
which is sometimes avoided in pregnancy due to theoretical risk of bleeding in the newborn (vitamin K
antagonism).

¢  Disulfiram-like reaction: Cefoperazone can cause a disulfiram-like reaction (flushing, nausea, headache) if
taken with alcohol; patients should avoid alcohol during treatment and for 3-5 days after.

8 Beta-Blockers: An Overview
Beta-blockers work by blocking the effects of the hormone epinephrine (adrenaline), which causes the heart to beat
slower and with less force, thereby reducing blood pressure. They are a diverse class of medications with varying
properties.

٠ Cardioselectivity (Beta-1 selective): Medications like Metoprolol, Bisoprolol, and Atenolol primarily target
0818-1 receptors in the heart. This makes them a preferred choice for many patients with certain respiratory
conditions (e.g., asthma) because they have less effect on the beta-2 receptors found in the lungs and blood
vessels, reducing the risk of bronchospasm.

٠ Non-Selective: Drugs such as Propranolol and Nadolo!l biock both beta-1 and beta-2 receptors, making
them effective for a broader range of conditions like tremors or migraine prevention, but they require more
caution in patients with asthma.

e Vasodilatory Beta-Blockers: Carvedilol and Labetalol possess additional alpha-blocking activity, which
causes blood vessels to widen (vasodilation). This property provides an added mechanism for lowering blood
Pressure and is particularly beneficial in treating chronic heart failure.

e Intrinsic Sympathomimetic Activity (ISA): Pindolol is a beta-blocker that also has a mild stimulating effect
on the heart, which can help prevent an excessive slowing of the heart rate (bradycardia), acommon side
effect of other drugs in this class.

--- Page 108 ---
e Key Uses: Beta-biockers are primarily used to manage cardiovascular conditions, which include:

o Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)

o Angina (Chest Pain)

o Heart Failure: To improve survival and reduce hospitalizations (specifically with drugs like

bisoprolol, carvedilol, and metoprolol succinate).

o Arrhythmias (irregular Heartbeats)

٠ Post-Myocardial Infarction (Heart Attack): To improve survival and prevent future attacks.

o Other Uses: They are also used for conditions like migraine prophylaxis, essential tremors, and

anxiety.

A Important Safety Note

The information above is for educational reference only. Beta-blockers are prescription medications and must be
used under the guidance of a qualified healthcare professional. Dosing, particularly for pediatric, elderly, or renally
impaired patients, must be individualized. Always consult a doctor and refer to the manufacturer's prescribing
information before making any medical decisions. A significant warning for beta-blockers is that they should never
be stopped abruptly, as this can lead to a rapid worsening of angina, a heart attack, or a dangerous increase in
blood pressure. The dosage must always be reduced gradually under medical supervision.

O Important Safety Note on CCBs

All caicium channel blockers can cause peripheral edema, headache, flushing, dizziness, and palpitations. Because
they reduce myocardial contractility and slow conduction, non-dihydropyridines (verapamil, diltiazem)

are contraindicated in patients with sick sinus syndrome (without a pacemaker), second- or third-degree AV biock,
hypotension, acute myocardial infarction with pulmonary congestion, and, particularly for verapamil, in patients
with left ventricular systolic dysfunction (LVEF <40%) or atrial fibrillation/flutter with an accessory bypass tract
(WPW). Dihydropyridines (amlodipine, nifedipine, felodipine) are generally safer in heart failure but may cause reflex
tachycardia and flushing. AH CCBs require dose adjustment in severe hepatic impairment. The information below is
for educational reference; always consult a qualified healthcare professional.

@ACE Inhibitors: An Overview
ACE inhibitors are a class of medications that work by relaxing and widening blood vessels, making it easier for the
heart to pump blood.

٠ Mechanism of Action: They inhibit the angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE), which is responsible for
producing a substance called angiotensin Il. Angiotensin || causes blood vessels to narrow and the body to
retain water. By blocking its production, ACE inhibitors help blood vessels relax and expand, reducing blood
pressure and improving blood flow. They are also commonly used to protect the kidneys in patients with
diabetes.

e Common Indications:

o Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)

o Heart Failure (Congestive Heart Failure, CHF)

٠ Post-Heart Attack Management (Left Ventricular Dysfunction)
o Diabetic Kidney Disease (Nephropathy)

o Prevention of Cardiovascular Events in high-risk patients

e Key Safety Considerations:

o Pregnancy: ACE inhibitors are associated with fetal toxicity and should be discontinued as soon as
pregnancy is detected.

--- Page 124 ---
o Renal Function: Requires monitoring as ACE inhibitors can impact kidney function.
o Potassium Levels: Can increase serum potassium levels; potassium supplements and potassium-
sparing diuretics should be used with caution.
٠ Angioedema: Although rare, can cause swelling of the face, lips, tongue, or throat, which is a medical
emergency. This history usually contraindicates future ACE inhibitor use.

o Cough: A persistent dry cough is a common side effect.
