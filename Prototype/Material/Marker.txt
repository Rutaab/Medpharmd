Severity tiers — practical definitions (used clinically)
For aminotransferases (ALT/AST) clinicians commonly use multiples of the upper limit of normal (ULN).
DynaMed/clinical references commonly describe:
٠ Mild: = 1-2 ULN (some sources: 2-5x ULN considered mild in other contexts)
٠ Moderate: 2-5x ULN (or 5-15x ULN in some classifications)
٠ Severe/massive: >5-15x ULN (very large rises seen in acute viral hepatitis, ischemic hepatitis) —
e.g., AST/ALT > 1000 U/L = severe/acute massive injury. (dynamed.com)
For ALP (cholestatic enzyme):
٠ Mild:<2* ULN
« Moderate: 2-4x ULN
« Marked/severe: >4x ULN (cholestasis/obstruction commonly gives 24x). StatPearls notes ALP 24x
ULN frequently occurs in cholestatic disease. (NCBI)
For GGT and 5'-NT: interpret relative to ALP— if ALP elevated and GGT or 5'-nucleotidase elevated, origin
is hepatic (not bone). No universal absolute cutoffs; use multiples of ULN: mild <3x, moderate 3-10x,
severe >10x as pragmatic bins.

Patterns of abnormality — how to read the panel
1. Hepatocellular (injury) pattern
o AST/ALT dominant (ALT usually > AST except alcohol): AST and ALT + (mild-severe).
o Suggests: viral hepatitis, toxin/drug injury, autoimmune hepatitis, ischemic hepatitis (very
high), NAFLD (mild). (NCBI)
2. Cholestatic (obstructive) pattern
o ALP? + 6617+ + conjugated bilirubine (ALP often 22-4x ULN).
o Suggests: extrahepatic obstruction (gallstone, tumor), intrahepatic cholestasis (PBC, PSC,
drugs). Use imaging/ultrasound. (NCBI)

3. Mixed pattern

o Moderate increases in both aminotransferases and ALP > mixed hepatocellular/cholestatic
causes (e.g., drug-induced liver injury, autoimmune disease, cholangitis).

4. Isolated bilirubin +

٠ Conjugated + > cholestasis or severe hepatocellular dysfunction.
o Unconjugated + > hemolysis, Gilbert syndrome, impaired conjugation.
5. Synthetic dysfunction pattern
o Lowalbumin + prolonged PT/INR- impaired synthetic capacity (chronic or acute severe
liver failure). PT/INR is the best short-term marker of synthetic failure. (Mayo Clinic)
Practical diagnostic approach (short)

1. Step 1— Pattern recognition: Are AST/ALT or ALP predominant? Check GGT and direct bilirubin to
confirm hepatic source. (NCBI)

2. Step 2— Severity: Use ULN multiples for AST/ALT and ALP to classify mild/moderate/severe. Very
high aminotransferases (> 1,000 U/L) > ischemia or toxin. (dynamed.com)

3. Step 3— Synthetic function: Check albumin and PTANR — if INR 21.5 or albumin low, consider
acute liver failure or chronic decompensation. (PMC)

4. Step 4— Targeted tests: Viral hepatitis serologies, ultrasound (for obstruction), autoimmune
markers, iron studies, ceruloplasmin, drug/toxin history.

5. Step 5— Manage/treat based on cause: e.g., ERCP for obstructive stone; N-acetylcysteine for
acetaminophen; supportive/ICU care for ischemic hepatitis.
