# Migraine

*Migraine is a "chronic neurological disorder characterized by recurrent, moderate to severe headaches that are typically independent, pulsating, and accompanied by devastating symptoms such as nausea, vomiting, photophobia (sensitivity to light), phonophobia (sensitivity to sound), and, in 20-30% of cases, aura (brief visual, sensory, or motor disturbances)."*

# A. Epidemiology

***Prevalence:**** Approximately "15% of the global population experiences migraine."*

***Age:**** It typically "begins during adolescence or early adulthood," peaking "between ages 25-55."*

***Gender:**** Women are significantly "more likely to experience migraine than men, with a female-to-male ratio of around 3:1."*

# B. Pathophysiology and Causes

*The exact causes are not fully understood, but several factors contribute:*

***Trigeminovascular System Activation:**** This system's activation leads to the "release of vasoactive neuropeptides like calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP), substance P, and neurokinin A, which cause inflammation and vasodilation of meningeal blood vessels, contributing to pain."*

***Neurotransmitter Imbalance:**** "Serotonin (5-HT) dysregulation" and changes in "other neurotransmitter levels" are contributors.*

***Central Sensitization:**** Occurs "in the brainstem and thalamus."*

***Genetics:**** "Family history plays a significant role, with many people having a first-degree relative with the condition."*

***Hormonal Changes:**** "Fluctuations in estrogen levels, particularly in women, can trigger migraines."*

***Environmental Triggers:**** "Stress, hormonal fluctuations, sleep disturbances, and certain foods exacerbate attacks." Other triggers include "changes in sleep patterns."*

# C. Symptoms

*Migraine symptoms vary but commonly include:*

***Headache:**** "A severe, throbbing or pulsating pain, often on one side of the head."*

***Aura:**** "Visual or sensory disturbances, such as flashing lights, zigzag patterns, or numbness"*

***Sensory Sensitivities:**** "Increased sensitivity to light and sound."*

***Gastrointestinal:**** "Nausea and vomiting" are common.*

***Other:**** "Fatigue, dizziness, and mood changes can also occur."*

# II. Anti-Migraine Drug Classifications and Treatments

*Anti-migraine drugs are categorized into two main types: ****acute (abortive) treatments**** and ****prophylactic (preventive) treatments****.*

## A. Acute (Abortive) Treatments

*These are "used to relieve symptoms during a migraine attack, targeting pain, nausea, and associated symptoms."*

### Triptans (Selective Serotonin Receptor (5-HT1B/1D) Agonists)

***Mechanism of Action:**** "Work by constricting blood vessels and blocking pain pathways in the brain. They bind to serotonin receptors, specifically 5-HT1B and 5-HT1D receptors, which leads to vasoconstriction and inhibition of pro-inflammatory neuropeptide release."*

***Therapeutic Uses:**** Treat acute migraine attacks, providing relief from headache, nausea, and sensitivity to light and sound.*

***Examples:***

***Sumatriptan (Imitrex):**** "Most effective when taken early in the attack, relieving headache, nausea, photophobia, and phonophobia within 2 hours in 60-70% of patients." Available in various forms, in oral tablets (25-100 mg), nasal spray (5-20 mg), subcutaneous injection (4-6 mg), and transdermal patches.*

***Rizatriptan (Maxalt):**** "Fast-acting triptan for severe migraine pain... achieves pain relief in 60-70% of patients within 2 hours."*

***Eletriptan (Relpax):**** "Effective for moderate to severe migraines."*

***Adverse Effects:**** Common (flushing, tingling, dizziness, fatigue); Serious (rare) include "**coronary vasospasm, myocardial ischemia, arrhythmias, and serotonin syndrome.**"*

***Contraindications:**** "Ischemic heart disease, coronary artery vasospasm, or peripheral vascular disease" due to vasoconstrictive effects. Also, concurrent use with ergots, other triptans, or MAO inhibitors.*

### Nonsteroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs)

***Mechanism of Action:**** "Inhibiting prostaglandin synthesis, which reduces inflammation and pain."*

***Therapeutic Uses:**** Treat "mild to moderate migraine attacks," often as a first-line treatment or in combination with triptans.*

***Examples:***

***Ibuprofen:**** "Effective for headache relief and associated symptoms, particularly in patients with infrequent attacks." Onset: 30-60 min, Duration: 4-6 hours.*

***Naproxen:**** Provides "longer-lasting analgesia than ibuprofen due to its extended half-life," suitable for moderate migraines needing prolonged relief. Onset: 1-2 hours, Duration: 6-12 hours.*

***Aspirin (Excedrin Migraine):**** "Effective for mild to moderate migraine pain."*

***Adverse Effects:**** Common (GI irritation, headache); Serious (GI bleeding, renal impairment, cardiovascular events with long-term use).*

***Contraindications:**** "Active peptic ulcer disease, severe renal or hepatic impairment, or hypersensitivity to NSAIDs."*

### Ergot Alkaloids (Ergots)

***History:**** "Derived from the ergot fungus, which has been used for centuries to treat various conditions, including migraine."*

***Mechanism of Action:**** "Constricting blood vessels and inhibiting pain pathways in the brain. They bind to serotonin receptors, which leads to vasoconstriction and inhibition of pro-inflammatory neuropeptide release."*

***Therapeutic Uses:**** Treat acute migraine attacks, "particularly in patients who do not respond to triptans."*

***Examples:***

***Ergotamine:**** Often "combined with caffeine to enhance absorption (e.g., Cafergot)."*

***Dihydroergotamine (D.H.E. 45 Injection, Migranal Nasal Spray):**** Available in injectable and nasal spray forms.*

***Side Effects:**** Common (nausea, vomiting, dizziness); Serious (ergotism, heart attack, stroke).*

***Contraindications:**** "Cardiovascular disease, peripheral vascular disease, uncontrolled hypertension, or pregnancy." Concurrent use with triptans or CYP3A4 inhibitors.*

### Antiemetics

*"Designed to prevent or lessen nausea and vomiting," commonly associated with migraine.*

***Mechanism of Action:**** "inhibiting the vomiting centers in the brain."*

***Examples:***

***Ondansetron (Zofran):**** "A serotonin 5-HT3 receptor antagonist."*

***Diphenhydramine (Benadryl):**** "An antihistamine."*

***Side Effects:**** Common (drowsiness, headache, constipation).*

***Contraindications:**** Ondansetron (gastrointestinal obstruction/perforation, hypersensitivity, long QT syndrome, concurrent apomorphine).*

### Ditans (Selective 5-HT1F Receptor Agonist)

***Lasmiditan:**** "Used for acute migraine with or without aura."*

***Key Feature:**** "Unlike triptans, it does not cause vasoconstriction, acting centrally to inhibit **trigeminal pain pathways** without affecting blood vessels, making it suitable for patients with cardiovascular contraindications."*

***Adverse Effects:**** Common (dizziness, somnolence, fatigue); Serious (rare) include serotonin syndrome, CNS depression (patients advised not to drive for 8 hours).*

***Contraindications:**** Hypersensitivity, severe hepatic impairment, concurrent use with serotonergic drugs.*

### Calcitonin Gene-Related Peptide (CGRP) Receptor Antagonists (Gepants)

***Mechanism of Action:**** "Block the action of calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP), a neuropeptide involved in migraine pathophysiology... preventing the peptide from exerting its effects," thereby "reducing migraine pain."*

***Therapeutic Uses:**** Treat acute migraine attacks, providing relief from headache, nausea, and sensitivity to light and sound.*

***Examples:***

***Rimegepant:**** "Blocks CGRP receptors, reducing migraine pain."*

***Ubrogepant:**** "Effective for acute migraine treatment."*

***Side Effects:**** Common (nausea, dry mouth, fatigue); Serious (hypersensitivity reactions, potential liver enzyme elevations).*

### Analgesics

***Purpose:**** "Designed to relieve pain."*

***Types:***

***NSAIDs:**** As described above, inhibit COX enzymes to reduce inflammation and pain.*

***Acetaminophen (paracetamol):**** "Believed to act primarily in the CNS by inhibiting a variant of the COX enzyme... reducing prostaglandin synthesis and thereby reducing pain perception." ****Has minimal/no anti-inflammatory effects****. "Used for mild migraine attacks, often in combination with aspirin and caffeine (e.g., Excedrin Migraine)."*

***Opioids:**** "Act by binding to opioid receptors (mu, delta, and **kappa)...** inhibit pain transmission by hyperpolarizing neurons... and reducing neurotransmitter release." Examples include morphine, fentanyl, and codeine.*

## B. Prophylactic (Preventive) Treatments

*These are "used to reduce the frequency, severity, and duration of migraine attacks, typically for patients with frequent (>4 attacks/month) or severe migraines."*

### Beta-Blockers

***Mechanism of Action:**** "Reducing blood vessel dilation and inhibiting pain pathways in the brain."*

***Therapeutic Uses:**** Prevent migraine attacks.*

***Examples:**** Propranolol, Metoprolol.*

***Side Effects:**** Common (fatigue, dizziness, shortness of breath); Serious (bradycardia, heart failure, bronchospasm).*

### Anticonvulsants

***Mechanism of Action:**** "Stabilizing electrical activity in the brain and inhibiting pain pathways."*

***Therapeutic Uses:**** Prevent migraine attacks.*

***Examples:**** Topiramate, Valproate.*

***Side Effects:**** Common (dizziness, nausea, fatigue); Serious (birth defects, liver damage, increased risk of suicidal thoughts and behaviors).*

### Antidepressants

***Mechanism of Action:**** "Regulating neurotransmitters, such as serotonin, which can contribute to migraine development."*

***Therapeutic Uses:**** Prevent migraine attacks.*

***Examples:**** Amitriptyline, Venlafaxine.*

***Side Effects:**** Common (nausea, dry mouth, increased appetite); Serious (increased risk of suicidal thoughts and behaviors, serotonin syndrome, withdrawal symptoms).*

### CGRP Monoclonal Antibodies

***Mechanism of Action:**** "Blocking the CGRP receptor, which reduces migraine frequency."*

***Therapeutic Uses:**** Prevent migraine attacks.*

***Examples:**** Erenumab (Aimovig).*

***Side Effects:**** Common (injection site reactions, muscle pain, hypersensitivity reactions); Serious (hypersensitivity reactions, potential increased risk of infections).*

### Other Preventive Treatments:

***Calcium Channel Blockers**** (e.g., verapamil)*

***Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE) Inhibitors/Angiotensin Receptor Blockers (ARBs)**** (e.g., lisinopril, candesartan)*
