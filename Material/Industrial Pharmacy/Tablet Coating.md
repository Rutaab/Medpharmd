# Tablet Coating

**Coating** is defined as the process of applying an **external layer** to the surface of a tablet. This layer is added specifically to impart **desired characteristics** that the core tablet may lack on its own.

# Objectives of Coating

The application of a coating serves several critical functions, ranging from patient compliance to chemical stability:

**Masking Unpleasant Traits:** It is used to mask the **taste, odor, or color** of the drug, making it more palatable for the patient.

**Protection:** It provides a barrier for both **physical and chemical protection** of the medicinal ingredients.

**Controlled Release:** Coating allows manufacturers to **control the release of the drug** from the tablet once ingested.

**Gastric Protection:** It protects sensitive drugs from the **acidic environment of the stomach** through the use of acid-resistant **enteric coatings**.

**Incompatibility Management:** A second drug can be incorporated into the coating to **avoid chemical incompatibilities** with the core ingredients or to facilitate a **sequential drug release**.

**Pharmaceutical Elegance:** It improves the **aesthetic appearance** of the tablet through the use of contrasting printings and special colors.

# Main Classes of Coating

Depending on the nature of the material applied and the process used, tablet coating is classified into three main categories:

## 1. Sugar Coating

This is one of the **oldest and most commonly used** processes in tablet manufacturing. It involves the application of **sucrose-based solutions** onto tablet cores using suitable equipment. The process is highly detailed and involves four main stages:

### a: Sealing (Waterproofing)

Sealing is the initial application of **water-insoluble materials** directly to the tablet core.

**Purpose:** Because the subsequent sugar-coating steps use aqueous (water-based) solutions, sealing is vital to **prevent moisture from penetrating** into the tablet core.

**Procedure:** Tablets are first placed in a coating pan and **dedusted** to remove loose powder from their surfaces.

**Materials:** Common sealing agents include **Shellac** or **CAP (Cellulose Acetate Phthalate)** dissolved in suitable solvents.

**Precautions:** Hot air is used to evaporate the solvent. It is important to limit this to **no more than two coats**, as excessive sealing can significantly **prolong the disintegration time** of the tablet.

**Alternative:** **Zein**, an alcohol-soluble protein, is often used as an alternative because it provides protection without affecting the disintegration time.

### b: Sub-coating

This is a critical stage where the tablet begins to take its final rounded shape.

**Purpose:** It helps to **round off the sharp edges** of the tablet core, builds up the overall **size of the tablet**, and improves the bond between the seal coat and the eventual sugar coat.

**Step 1 (Binding):** A concentrated solution of **sticky binders**, such as gelatin or gum acacia, is applied to the tablet bed until the tablets begin to stick together. They are then partially dried using hot air.

**Step 2 (Powdering):** Sub-coating powders--which may include **fine sugar, starch, talc****, or calcium carbonate (CaCO**~**3**~**)**--are dusted over the sticky tablets until they become free-moving again.

**Repetition:** These two steps are repeated alternately until the tablets reach the **desired volume and shape**.

### c: Smoothing and Colouring

After sub-coating, the tablet surface is typically rough and requires refinement before the final finish.

**Smoothing:** Concentrated **sugar syrup** (with or without gum acacia) is applied several times until the surfaces are smoothed to the desired degree. This step is essential to prevent "mottling" (uneven color) during the next phase.

**Colouring:** Colorants are mixed with a simple syrup. The process begins with a **dilute concentration** of color, which is **gradually increased** through successive applications until the exact target color is achieved.

### d: Polishing

Polishing is the final step of the sugar-coating process.

**Purpose:** This stage is strictly aesthetic, used to obtain the **desired luster or gloss** on the tablet surface.

**Equipment:** It is performed in clean, standard coating pans or specialized **canvas-lined polishing pans**.

**Methods:** 

**Dry Application:** Powdered wax, such as **beeswax or carnauba wax**, is sprinkled over the tumbling tablets until they shine.

**Solution Application:** Occasionally, a **warm wax solution** (using solvents like naphtha or chloroform) is sprayed over the tablet bed. The solvents are evaporated while the pan continues to rotate until the tablets reach their peak luster.

## 2. Film Coating

Film coating is a modern pharmaceutical technique characterized by the deposition of a **thin and uniform polymeric organic coating** onto the surface of a tablet core.

### Advantages over Sugar Coating

Introduced in the 1960s to overcome the inherent limitations of traditional sugar coating, film coating offers several significant benefits:

**Efficiency:** The process is more **economical** and significantly **less time-consuming** than the multi-stage sugar-coating process.

**Physical Properties:** Film-coated tablets maintain the **same weight** as the original tablet core, whereas sugar coating adds substantial bulk.

**Durability:** These tablets are **more resistant to abrasion** during handling and shipping.

**Functional Features:** The thin nature of the film allows tablets to be **easily engraved** with trademarks or logos.

**Patient Compliance:** Overall, film coating results in **better patient compliance** compared to sugar-coated alternatives.

### The Film Coating Process

The process is streamlined and involves a few key steps:

A batch of tablets is placed into a **coating pan**.

The **coating formulation** (solution or suspension) is **sprayed** over the rotating tablet bed.

The **solvent is evaporated** using heated air, leaving the film adhered to the tablet core.

### Composition of Film Coating Solution

A film coating solution is composed of several functional constituents that ensure the film is durable, flexible, and aesthetically pleasing.

**Film-forming Polymers:** These are the **main components** responsible for creating the physical film over the tablet core. An ideal polymer is one that is soluble in a wide range of solvents; **C.A.P (Cellulose Acetate Phthalate)** is a commonly used example.

**Alloying Substances:** These agents are added to **facilitate water penetration** through the film and into the tablet core. This is necessary to ensure the tablet can undergo **disintegration and dissolution** once ingested. **Polyethylene glycol** is a typical alloying substance.

**Plasticizers:** These materials, such as **castor oil**, are incorporated to **increase the flexibility and elasticity** of the film, preventing it from becoming too brittle.

**Surfactants (Detergents):** These agents are used to **improve the spreadability** of the film solution over the surface of the tablet core. **Polyoxyethylene sorbitol derivatives** are frequently used for this purpose.

**Colourants:** These impart characteristic colors to the final product. Manufacturers often use **F, D, & C (Food, Drug, and Cosmetic) dyes**. Additionally, **titanium dioxide** is used to provide **opacity** to the film.

**Flavours and Aromas:** These are included to **improve the taste** and smell of the medication, thereby increasing patient acceptability. Examples include **saccharine and vanillin**.

**Solvents:** Solvents are the vehicles used to apply the coating constituents. While organic solvents were traditionally used, modern trends favor **aqueous (water-based) solvents** because they are **safer and cheaper**. Common solvents used alone or in combination include: 

Water

Ethanol and Methanol

Chloroform and Acetone

Methyl ethyl ketone and Methylene chloride

## Sugar vs Film Coating

**Process Efficiency **

**Complexity:** Sugar coating is a laborious multi-stage process involving sealing, sub-coating, smoothing, coloring, and polishing. In contrast, film coating involves spraying a polymeric formulation over a tablet bed and evaporating the solvent in a more streamlined fashion.

**Physical Characteristics**

**Weight and Size:** Sugar coating involves a "sub-coating" stage specifically designed to **build up the tablet size** and round off edges, which adds substantial weight. Film coating produces a **thin and uniform layer** that maintains the **same weight** as the original tablet core.

**Durability:** Film-coated tablets are **more resistant to abrasion** than sugar-coated ones.

**Engraving:** Because the film is so thin, film-coated tablets can be **easily engraved** with trademarks or logos, a feature that improves patient compliance and brand identification.

**Composition and Materials**

**Coating Base:** Sugar coating relies on **sucrose-based solutions** and various powders like starch and calcium carbonate. Film coating uses **polymeric organic coatings** composed of film-forming polymers (such as Cellulose Acetate Phthalate), plasticizers, and surfactants.

**Solvents:** While sugar coating primarily uses aqueous sucrose solutions (requiring a "sealing" coat to protect the core from water), film coating can use either **organic solvents** or **aqueous solvents**.

**Patient Compliance**

**Acceptability:** Film coating is noted for having **better patient compliance** compared to the older sugar-coating method. While sugar coating is excellent at masking taste through sweetness, film coating offers a more modern, durable, and functional finish.

## 2a. Enteric Coating

**Enteric coating** is described as a specialized form of film coating designed to keep a dosage form **intact within the stomach**, ensuring that the medicinal contents are only released once the tablet reaches the **small intestine**.

### Objectives of Enteric Coating

There are five primary reasons why a manufacturer would choose to use an enteric coating:

**Drug Sensitivity:** It is applied when a drug is sensitive to certain pH levels, specifically to protect it from the **gastric environment** of the stomach.

**Gastric Protection:** It protects the **gastric mucosa** (stomach lining) from potential adverse effects or irritation caused by the drug.

**Optimal Absorption:** It is used when a drug is **best absorbed in the intestine** rather than the stomach.

**Local Action:** It is necessary when the drug is intended for **local action within the intestine** itself.

**Release Control:** It is utilized when a **sustained or repeat action** of the medication is required.

### Techniques for Enteric Coating

To ensure the coating performs its function correctly--remaining stable in the stomach but dissolving in the intestine--two main techniques are employed:

**Increased Thickness:** The thickness of the coating is increased to a specific level where it will **not dissolve in the gastric environment**.

**pH Sensitivity:** The coating is formulated to be **sensitive to basic (alkaline) pH**, allowing it to dissolve specifically once it encounters the environment of the intestine.

**Polymers Used for Enteric Coating**

Several specific polymers and mixtures are used to create these acid-resistant barriers:

**Cellulose acetate phthalate (C.A.P)**

**Polyvinyl acetate phthalate (P.V.A.P)**

**Hydroxypropyl methyl cellulose phthalate (H.P.M.C.P)**

**Polyvinyl Pyrolidone phthalate (P.P.P)**

**Specialized Mixture:** A combination consisting of **45% n-butyl stearate**, **30% Carnauba wax**, and **25% Stearic acid**.

## Common Coating Defects and Remedies

### Picking and Sticking

**Description & Cause:** Tablets stick to each other or to the coating pan, causing portions of the film to be removed. This is caused by an **excessive application rate** of the coating solution or **inadequate drying**.

**Remedy:** Reduce the application rate of the solution and **increase the temperature or velocity** of the drying air.

### Roughness

**Description & Cause:** The film takes on a rough appearance because drops of the coating solution **dry partially** before they actually reach the surface of the tablet.

**Remedy:** **Decrease the distance** between the spraying nozzle and the tablet bed.

### Orange Peel Effect

**Description & Cause:** The tablet develops a **bumpy appearance** resembling an orange peel due to improper spreading of the coating solution.

**Remedy:** **Increase the concentration of plasticizers** in the coating formulation.

### Bridging

**Description & Cause:** This occurs when the film **stretches or shrinks** away from the sharp corners of a monogram or logo, creating a bridge-like shape over the intended indentation.

**Remedy:** Increase the quantity of **solvent or plasticizers**.

### Filling

**Description & Cause:** This happens when an over-application of the coating solution **fills in depressions** like breaking lines or monograms on the tablet.

**Remedy:** Ensure **proper monitoring** of the spraying process.

### Blistering

**Description & Cause:** High temperatures during the drying process cause evaporation from the tablet core, creating internal stress that **detaches the film** from the core.

**Remedy:** Conduct the drying process at a **lower temperature**.

### Dull Effect

**Description & Cause:** A lack of luster often caused by **high processing temperatures** (especially when using cellulosic polymers in aqueous solvents) or storage in **high humidity**.

**Remedy:** Use lower processing temperatures and ensure the tablets are **stored in low humidity**.

### Colour Variation

**Description & Cause:** An uneven distribution of color on the tablet surface caused by **improper mixing**, uneven spray conditions, or dye migration during drying.

**Remedy:** Ensure **proper mixing** and use **mild drying** conditions.

### Film Cracking

**Description & Cause:** The film may crack due to **internal stress**.

**Remedy:** Use **high molecular weight polymers** that possess high tensile strength.

3. **Compression Coating**
**Compression coating** is a specialized and relatively rare pharmaceutical process where a coating is applied to a tablet through mechanical pressure rather than the application of a liquid solution.

The process and its key characteristics include:

**"Tablet within a Tablet":** This method essentially creates a multi-layered dosage form where a pre-compressed inner core is surrounded by an outer layer of coating material.

**Specialized Equipment:** Because this process does not involve spraying or pans, it requires a **special compression machine** designed to center the core tablet within the coating material before final compression.

**Key Use Cases:** This method is specifically selected for two main reasons: 

**Solvent Sensitivity:** It is used when the coating material is **highly sensitive to aqueous or organic solvents**, making traditional sugar or film coating impossible.

**Ingredient Incompatibility:** It allows for the physical **separation of ingredients** that would otherwise react with each other if mixed in a single core. This enables the inclusion of chemically incompatible drugs within the same tablet.

# Coating Equipments

The equipment used for tablet coating is generally known as **coating pans**, which are divided into three main types based on their design and how they dry the tablets.

## 1: Standard Coating Pan (Conventional Pan)

A standard coating pan consists of a **circular metal pan** (usually 6 to 80 inches in diameter) that sits at an angle on a stand. A motor rotates the pan on its horizontal axis while a batch of tablets moves inside it.

**Process:** Heated air is introduced to the pan to dry the tablets while the coating solution is either poured or, more commonly, **sprayed using atomizers** onto the rotating tablet bed. The used air is then removed through ducts at the front of the pan.

**Modifications for Better Efficiency**

Because standard pans can sometimes be slow at drying, several modifications have been developed to improve air contact and solution application:

**Pellegrini Coating Pan:** This uses a **baffled pan** (with strips on the inner surface) and a **diffuser** that spreads the drying air evenly across the tablets. Some newer models are fully enclosed to be even more efficient.

**Immersion Sword Coating Pan:** A perforated, sword-like metal device is **pushed directly into the tablet bed**. Drying air flows out of this sword through the tablets, creating a very efficient drying environment because the air and wet tablets are mixed closely.

**Immersion Tube Coating Pan:** A tube is dipped into the tablet bed and **simultaneously delivers** both the heated air and the coating solution. This system is known for processing both film and sugar coatings very quickly.

## 2: Perforated Coating Pan

In these systems, the coating pan itself has small holes (**perforations**) to make the drying process much faster. Hot air typically passes through the bed of tablets and is pulled out through the other side of the pan.

**Accela**** Coater:** This is a fully enclosed system where drying air is directed into the pan, passed through the tablet bed, and then **exhausted through the holes** in the pan.

**Hi-Coater:** This machine uses the **same basic principle** as the Accela Coater but has a different physical equipment design.

**Dria**** Coater:** This system has **hollow perforated ribs** located on the inside edges of the drum. As the pan rotates, these ribs dip into the tablet bed and release drying air, which **fluidizes the bed** before being exhausted out the back of the pan.

## 3: Fluidized Bed Coating Pan (Air Suspension Coater)

This is a highly efficient system that looks like a tall column rather than a round pan.

**Mechanism:** Instead of rotating the tablets in a pan, a powerful **upward flow of drying air** lifts the tablets into the air (a process called **fluidization** or suspension).

**Coating Application:** While the tablets are suspended in the air, the coating solution is sprayed from **nozzles** located either at the **top** of the chamber or at the **bottom**. In some units, a smaller inner column is used to help guide the movement of the tablets within the main chamber.
