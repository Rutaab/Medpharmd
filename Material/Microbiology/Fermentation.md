# Fermentation


“The chemical breakdown of a substance by bacteria, yeasts, or other microorganisms, typically involving effervescence and the giving off of heat is called as fermentation.” Anaerobic enzymatically controlled biological process in which organic compounds are converted into simple ones Effervescence takes place along with release of heat But now it is defined as large scale cultivation of microorganisms whether aerobic or anaerobic (without O2)

### Types of Fermentation:

Aerobic (in presence of O2)

Anaerobic (in absence of O2)

Aerobic Fermentation: Adequate aeration adequate supply of sterile air Antibiotics, enzymes, vitamins.

Anaerobic Fermentation: In anaerobic fermentation, a provision for aeration is usually not needed. Lactic acid, ethanol, wine

### Types of Fermentation Processes:

Fermentation could be of two following process types 1. Batch mode 2. Fed batch mode (continuous fermentation) **1.** **Batch fermentation: ** Most fermentations are batch processes Nutrients and the inoculum are added to the sterile fermenter and left to get on with it! Anti-foaming agent may be added. Once the desired amount of product is present in the fermenter the contents are drained off and the product is extracted. After emptying, the tank is cleaned & prepared for a new batch. **2.** **Continuous fermentation: ** Some products are made by a continuous culture system. Sterile medium is added to the fermentation with a balancing withdrawal of broth for product extraction.

**ANTIBIOTICS BY FERMENTATION ** Of all the microbial products manufactured commercially, antibiotics are the most important. Antibiotics are chemical substances produced by microorganisms to kill or inhibits the growth of other microorganisms. They are used in the treatment of infectious diseases.

### Production of Antibiotics

**Antibiotics: ** ” Substance produced by one microorganism which in low concentration inhibit the growth of other microorganisms.” **Sources: ** 1-Microorganisms 2-Semi-synthetic 3-Synthetic **1.** ** Microorganisms:** E.g. Bacitracin and polymyxin from Bacillus species. Streptomycin tetracyclines etc. from Streptomyces species. Gentamicin from Micromonospora purpurea. Penicillins and cephalosporins from certain genera ( Penicillium acremonium ) family Aspergillaceae. **2.** **Semi-synthetic** : Chemical synthesis that uses compounds isolated from natural source. Usually done through fermentation process. Product after fermentation is modified through chemical process to produce bulk antibiotics or Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient (API). The bulk antibiotic or API is formulated into required dosage form e.g. tablets vials injections etc. There are 2 stages for Semi-synthetic process o **Primary** **manufacture** : Stages up to API o **Secondary** **manufacture** : Stages from API to formulated product. **3.** **Synthetic:** Chemotherapeutic or antimicrobial agents of synthetic origin. **E.g.** Chloramphenicol is usually produced by Synthetic process.

### Steps involved in Antibiotic preparation:

The steps involved in the preparation of antibiotics are: **1.** **The organism:** The original organism is required for the production of antibiotics. **2.** **Inoculum preparation:** The aim is to develop a pure inoculum in sufficient volume & fast growing phase so that high population density is quickly achieved. Adequate oxygen is provided in the form of sterile air and temperature is controlled at desired level.

**3.** **Fermenter :** An apparatus used for fermentation process. **4.** **Control of Fermentation:** Following are the conditions required for controlled fermentation. o **Batched medium: ** The batch medium contains nitrogen sources and additional nutrients such as calcium magnesium, sulphate, phosphate trace metals. o **Fed nutrients :** Sucrose or glucose, molasses starch hydrolysate and rice etc. o **Termination.**

### FERMENTER:

“An apparatus that maintains optimal conditions for the growth of microorganism used in large scale fermentation.” A typical fermenter is a closed vertical cylindrical stainless steel vessel with convexly dished ends and of 25-250m 3 capacity . Its height is usually two to three times in diameter. **Process that takes place in fermenter: ** **Oxygen supply:** o Impellers and baffles assist the transfer of oxygen and promotes mixing of cells and nutrients. o Impellers are mounted on a rotating vertical stirrer shaft which is mechanically driven by a poweful electric motor. **Temperature control:** o Heat transfer is achieved by circulating chilled water through banks of pipes inside vessel. o Cooling water system is also used to cool batched medium.

**Defoaming agents and instrumentation:** o Instrumentation provides display of important variables e.g temperature, pH & 0 2 consumed etc. o An analysis of exhaust gases provides physiological information. **Media addition: ** o Media addition by fed batch process takes place. **Transfer and sampling system:** o Aseptic system are used to transfer the inoculum to vessel o Asepsis is ensured by engineering design and by steam. o Sampling is essential to monitor amount of growth and running level of key nutrients. **Some Antibiotics produced by Microorganisms **

### Production of Penicillin By Fermentation

**MOA of Penicillin: ** All penicillin like antibiotics inhibit synthesis of peptidoglycan, an essential part of the cell wall. They do not interfere with the synthesis of other intracellular components. These antibiotics do not affect human cells because human cells do not have cell walls. **Spectrum of Activity: ** Penicillins are active against Gram positive bacteria Some members (e.g. amoxicillin) are also effective against Gram negative bacteria but not Pseudomonas aeruginosa **Production of Penicillin: ** Penicillin was the first important commercial product produced by an aerobic, submerged First antibiotic manufactured in bulk. Used as input material for some semi synthetic antibiotics. It is fermented in a batch culture. **Primary Metabolites: ** Primary metabolites are produced during active cell growth **Secondary Metabolites: ** Secondary metabolites are produced near the onset of stationary phase. Like all antibiotics, penicillin is a secondary metabolite, so is only produced in the stationary phase

### Industrial Production of Antibiotic- Penicillin

The industrial production of penicillin was broadly classified in to two processes namely, 1. **Upstream processing ** 2. **Downstream processing**

### 1. Upstream Processing:

Upstream processing encompasses any technology that leads to the synthesis of a product. Upstream includes the exploration, development and production. Inoculum Preparation: o The medium is designed to provide the organism with all the nutrients that it requires. o Inoculation method- submerged technique o Spores -major source of inoculum **Raw Materials: ** o **Carbon Sources:** Lactose acts as a very satisfactory carbon compound, provided that is used in a concentration of 6%. Others such as glucose & sucrose may be used. o **Nitrogen Sources:** Corn steep liquor (CSL) Ammonium sulphate and ammonium acetate can be used as nitrogenous sources. o **Mineral Sources:** Elements namely potassium, phosphorus, magnesium, sulphur, zinc and copper are essential for penicillin production. Some of these are applied by corn steep liquor. **Calcium** can be added in the form of chalk to counter the natural acidity of CSL **PAA** precursor **Fermentation Process: ** o The medium is inoculated with a suspension of conidia of Penicillium chrysogenum. o The medium is constantly aerated and agitated, and the mould grows throughout as pellets. o After about seven days, growth is complete, the pH rises to 8.0 or above, and penicillin production ceases

### 2. Downstream Process:

The extraction and purification of a biotechnological product from fermentation is referred to as downstream processing. **Stages in Downstream Processing: ** **Removal of cells ** o The first step in product recovery is the separation of whole cells and other insoluble ingredients from the culture broth by technique such as filtration and centrifugation. **Isolation of Benzyl Penicillin: ** o The PH is adjusted to 2-2.5 with the help of phosphoric or sulphuric acids. o This step has to be carried out quickly for penicillin is very unstable at low pH values. Antibiotic is then extracted back into an aqueous buffer at a pH of 7.5, the partition coefficient now being strongly in favor of the aqueous phase. The resulting aqueous solution is again acidified & re-extracted with an organic solvent. These shifts between the water and solvent help in the purification of penicillin. The treatment of the crude penicillin extract varies according to the objective, but involves the formation of an appropriate penicillin salt. The solvent extract recovered in the previous stage is carefully extracted back with aqueous sodium hydroxide. This is followed by charcoal treatment to eliminate pyrogens and by sterilization. Pure metal salts of penicillin can be safely sterilized by dry heat, if desired. Thereafter, the aqueous solution of penicillin is subjected to crystallization. **3.** **Further Processing: ** For parental use, the antibiotic is packed in sterile vials as a powder or suspension.

**The Main Stages of Penicillin Production are: ** 1. A medium of corm steep liquor (a by product of starch manufacture), yeast extract and other substrates are added in the fermenter. 2. After 40 hours, Penicillin begins to be secreted by the fungus. 3. The mould mycelium (cell matter) is filtered from the harvested product. 4. Penicillin is extracted in the organic solvent; butylacetate, in which it dissolves. 5. Potassium salts are added and a penicillium precipitate is formed. This is washed and dried.

Searching tests (ex: for purity, potency) are performed on the appreciable number of random samples of the finished product.
