## Chemical Method of Sterilization

“Chemical sterilization refers to a technique of sterilization by making use of a chemical agent.” Chemical sterilization is typically used for devices that would be sensitive to the high heat used in steam sterilization, and for devices that may be damaged by irradiation (rubbers and plastics

### can become more brittle after irradiation.)

**Types of Chemical Sterilization: ** Chemical Sterilization can be of several types: 1. Sterilization by Gases 2. Sterilization by Liquids **1.** **Sterilization by Gases: ** Sterilizing gases are typically used when exposure to other methods (heat or radiation)

### could damage the materials or equipment.

The most common gases used for sterilization are: i. Ethylene Oxide ii. Ozone

**i.** Ethylene Oxide: Ethylene oxide (EO or EtO) gas is commonly used to sterilize objects sensitive to temperatures greater than 60 °C and/or radiation such as plastics, optics and electrics. **Standards for EtO: ** A clean object can be sterilized if treated for 5-8 hours at 38°C or 3-4 hours at 54°C Gas concentration between 200 and 800 mg/l Relative humidity should be 40-70% Because pure EtO is explosive, it is usually supplied in a 10 to 20% concentration mixed with either CO 2 or dichlorodifluoromethane ** Precaution ** Extensive aeration of sterilized materials is necessary to remove residual EtO because it is so toxic. ** Mode of action ** EtO is a very strong alkylating agent, and it reacts vigorously with functional groups of DNA and proteins. Through these actions, it blocks both DNA replication and enzymatic actions **Sterilization power:** Ethylene oxide penetrates well, moving through paper, cloth, and some plastic films and is highly effective EtO can kill all known viruses, bacteria and fungi, including bacterial spores. **Applications: **

EtO is an effective way to sterilize and disinfect plastic materials and delicate instruments in hospitals and industries.

EtO can safely sterilize prepackaged medical devices and surgical supplies, syringes, and disposable petri plates.

**Disadvantage: ** One of its disadvantages is its comparatively slow action upon microorganisms

It is highly flammable.

It is highly toxic.

It is carcinogenic.

**2.** **Sterilization by Liquids: ** “Liquid Chemical Sterilization is used to sterilize heat sensitive devices which can be immersed.”

The method requires the item to be completely immersed in a germicidal solution for a prescribed period of time to kill all the bacterial micro-organisms.

Liquid chemical sterilization involves a two-part process: Devices are treated with a liquid chemical germicide (LCG). The processed devices are rinsed with water to remove the chemical residues.

The liquids used for sterilizing are as follows: i. Chlorine Bleach ii. Glutaraldehyde and Formaldehyde iii. Phthaldehyde iv. Hydrogen Peroxide

**i.** Chlorine Bleach: It is another accepted liquid sterilizing agent. Household bleach consists of 6.25% sodium hypochlorite. **Concentration Used: ** It is usually diluted to 1/10 immediately before use; however, to kill Mycobacterium tuberculosis it should be diluted only 1/5, and 1/2.5 to inactivate prions. For water it is used 0.6-1 ppm **Time: ** Bleach will kill many organisms immediately, but for full sterilization it should be allowed to react for 20 minutes. **Mode of action ** Oxidation In solution, these compounds combine with water and release hypochlorous acid (HOCl) and then atomic oxygen [O], which oxidizes the sulfhydryl (S-H) group on amino acid cysteine and interferes with disulfide (S-S) bridges on numerous enzymes **Disadvantage: ** Bleach will kill many, but not all spores. It is also highly corrosive. Bleach decomposes over time when exposed to air, so fresh solutions should be made daily.

**ii.** Glutaraldehyde and Formaldehyde: Solutions are accepted liquid sterilizing agents. To kill all spores in a clear liquid can take up to 22 hours with glutaraldehyde and even longer with formaldehyde. Glutaraldehyde and formaldehyde are volatile, and toxic by both skin contact and inhalation. **Disadvantage: ** Long duration for sterilization Causes prions resistance to itself and also to other chemical sterilants.

**iii.** Phthalaldehyde: Ortho-phthalaldehyde (OPA) is a chemical sterilant. Typically used in a 0.55% solution, high-level disinfectant at an immersion time of 12 minutes at 20°C and 5 minutes at 25°C. OPA shows better myco-bactericidal activity than glutaraldehyde. Also effective against glutaraldehyde-resistant spores. OPA has superior stability, is less volatile, and does not irritate skin or eyes, and it acts more quickly than glutaraldehyde. **Mode of action: ** Forms covalent cross-links with functional groups of proteins. **Disadvantages: ** It is more expensive and will stain proteins (including skin) gray in color. Some side effects from equipment sterilized using this reagent have been reported. For example, two cases of anaphylaxis following cystoscopy with endoscopes sterilized with OPA were reported

**iv.** Hydrogen peroxide: Hydrogen peroxide (H 2 O 2 ) is a colourless, caustic liquid that decomposes in the presence of light, metals, or catalase into water and oxygen gas. Early formulations were unstable and inhibited by organic matter, but manufacturing methods now permit synthesis of hydrogen peroxide so stable that even dilute solutions retain activity through several months of storage. **Mode of action: ** Strong oxidizing agent The germicidal effects of hydrogen peroxide are due to the direct and indirect actions of oxygen. Oxygen forms hydroxyl free radicals (OH), which, like the superoxide radical, are highly toxic and reactive to cells. **Microbicidal Activity: ** Hydrogen peroxide is bactericidal, virucidal and fungicidal and in higher conc. sporicidal. **Concentration used: ** Mostly 0.2-3%. In medical sterilization hydrogen peroxide is used at higher concentrations, ranging from around 35% up to 90%. **Time for sterilization: ** Up to 28-30 min **Materials sterilized: ** Hospital Endoscopes Catheters

## DISINFECTION

“The process of cleaning something, especially with a chemical in order to destroy bacteria.” Disinfection does not necessarily kill all microorganisms, especially resistant bacterial spores; it is less effective than sterilization, which is an extreme physical and/or chemical process that kills all types of life. **Disinfectant: ** “Disinfectants are antimicrobial agents that are applied to the surface of non-living objects to destroy microorganisms that are living on the objects.” Disinfectants are different from other antimicrobial agents such as antibiotics, which destroy microorganisms within the body, and antiseptics, which destroy microorganisms on living tissue

### Types of Disinfection

There are two types of disinfection: Physical Methods Chemical Methods

### Physical Methods of Disinfection:

Physically disinfection can be done by following methods: 1. Direct Boiling 2. Pasteurization

### 1. Direct Boiling:

Objects can be disinfected by boiling it directly. Boiling can kill the microorganisms present on the objects. e.g. Water is disinfected by boiling.

### 2. Pasteurization:

“Pasteurization is a process of heating a food, usually a liquid, to a specific temperature for a definite length of time and then cooling it immediately.” This process slows microbial growth in the food. Pasteurization aims to reduce the number of viable pathogens, so they are unlikely to cause disease **Methods of Pasteurization: ** There are three methods of Pasteurization:

**i.** Long Time Method: In this method the pasteurization is brought about by heating at 63 o C for 30 minutes.

**ii.** High Temperature/Short Time Method: In this method pasteurization is achieved by heating at 71.7 o C for 15-20 seconds. **Ultra-High Temperature:** In this method pasteurization is done at 140 o C to

**iii.** 150oC for 3 to 1 seconds

### Chemical Methods of Disinfection:

Disinfection can also be brought about by chemical means using chemical reagents which are used to kill microorganisms.

### 1. Alcohols:

Alcohols are protein denaturants, and accounts for their antimicrobial activity. Alcohols are also solvents for lipids, and hence they may damage lipid complexes in cell membrane. They are also dehydrating agents.

**i.** Methyl alcohol: o Less bactericidal than ethyl alcohol; furthermore, it is highly poisonous. Even the fumes of this compound may produce permanent injury to the eyes, and is not generally employed for the destruction of microorganisms

**ii.** Ethyl Alcohol: o In conc. between 50 and 90%, is effective against vegetative or non- sporeforming cells. For practical applications 70% of alcohol is generally used

**iii.** The Higher Alcohols (Propyl, Butyl, Amyl, and others): o More germicidal than ethyl alcohol. In fact, there is a progressive increase in germicidal power as the molecular weight of alcohols increases (Since alcohols of molecular weight higher than that of propyl alcohol are not miscible in all proportions with water, they are not commonly used as disinfectants). Propyl and isopropyl alcohols in conc. ranging from 40-80% are bactericidal for vegetative cells. **Applications of Alcohol: ** o Alcohol is effective in reducing the microbial flora of skin and for the disinfection of clinical oral thermometers o Ethyl alcohol is germicidal, non-irritating, and inexpensive. Solution of 70-95% are routinely used as skin degerming agent because the surfactant action removes skin oil and some microbes sheltered in deeper skin layers (One limitation to its effectiveness is the rate at which it evaporates) o Ethyl alcohol is occasionally used to disinfect electrodes, face masks, and thermometers which are first cleaned and then soaked in alcohol for 15-20 minutes. Isopropyl alcohol, sold as rubbing alcohol, is even more microbicidal and less expensive than ethanol

### 4. Aldehydes:

Two of the most effective are formaldehyde and glutaraldehyde. Both are highly microbicidal, and both have the ability to kill spores (sporicidal).

**i.** Formaldehyde: o It is a gas that is stable only in high conc. and at elevated temperatures. At room temperatures it polymerizes, forming a solid substance. □Formaldehyde is an extremely reactive chemical. It combines readily with vital organic nitrogen compounds such as proteins, nucleic acids. It is likely that interaction of formaldehyde with these cellular substances accounts for its antimicrobial actions.

Mechanism of action:

□The mechanism of activity involves cross linking protein molecules on the cell surface. In this process, amino acids are alkylated, meaning that a hydrogen atom on an amino acid is replaced by glutaraldehyde molecule itself. It can also irreversibly disrupt the activity of enzymes within the cell

Application:

□Glutaraldehyde is rapid and broad spectrum, and is one of the few chemicals officially accepted as a sterilant and high level disinfectant. It kills spores in 3 hours and fungi and vegetative bacteria in a few minutes. Viruses, including the most resistant forms, appear to be inactivated after relatively short exposure times

### 5. Alkalis and acids:

o Propionic acid is commonly incorporated into breads and cakes to retard mold. o Benzoic and sorbic acids are added to beverages, syrups and margarine to inhibit yeast.

### 6. Heavy metals:

### 9. Halogens:

The halogens iodine and chlorine are important antimicrobial agents **Iodine:** Mostly used as antiseptic **Chlorine:** Chlorine has been used for disinfection and antisepsis for approximately 200 years. The major forms used in microbial control are liquid and gaseous chlorine (Cl 2 ), hypochlorite (OCl), and chloramines (NH 2 Cl). **Mechanism of action:** o In solution, these compounds combine with water and release hypochlorous acid (HOCl) and then atomic oxygen [O], which oxidizes the sulfhydryl (S-H) group on amino acid cysteine and interferes with disulfide (S-S) bridges on numerous enzymes.
