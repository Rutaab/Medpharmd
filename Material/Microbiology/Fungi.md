# Fungi


**Introduction ** Fungus is a member of a large group of eukaryotic organisms that includes microorganisms such as yeasts and molds, as well as more familiar mushrooms. Fungi are eukaryotic absorptive heterotrophs. There cell wall is made up of chitin.

### Classification of Fungi

Fungi are classified as follows: 1. Zygomycota 2. Ascomycota 3. Basidiomycota 4. Deuteromcota **1.** **Zygomycota: ** Also called Zygomycetes During their sexual reproduction, zygote formed directly by the fusion of hyphae forms temporary, dormant, thick walled resistant structures called zygospores. Hence the name Zygomycetes. Asexual reproduction by spores is common. Hyphae are coenocytic. **Examples: ** Rhizopus and Mucor etc. **2.** **Ascomycota: ** Also called ascomycetes. They produce haploid sexual spores called ascospores by meiosis inside their characteristic sac like structures calles asci (sing. ascus). It is the largest group of fungi. Hyphae are septate. **Example:** Blastomyces, histoplasma, microsporum, trichophyton, coccidioides and yeasts ( saccharomyces and candida )

**3.** **Basidiomycota: ** Also called basidiomycetes. These are named so for their characteristic, club- shaped (hence also called club-fungi) sexual structures called the **basidium** (plural. basidia). These are among the most familiar fungi; edible fungi, devastating plant pathogens etc. **Example:** Mushrooms and cryptococcus . **4.** **Deuteromycota: ** Also called deuteromycetes. This heterogenous group includes all such fungi in which the sexual phase has not been observed. Most of them are related to their sexually reproducing relatives of ascomycetes, however some are related to other two phyla as well. These are also called imperfect fungi. **Example:** Penicillium, aspergillus and fungi causing athlete's foot, ringworm etc.

### Terminologies:

A few terminologies related to fungi are explained as below: **Yeast ** Unicellular form that are spherical or ovoid in shape **Molds: ** Complex multicellular microorganisms having variety of specialized structuctures with specific functions **Hyphae ** Thread like tubes containing cytoplasm and organelles of organism **Septa ** Cross walls in hyphae forming individual cells **Conidiophore or Sporangiophores ** Specialized hypha that bears reproductive structures of some molds **Conidia or spore ** Conidia are asexual fungal spores. **Diamorphs ** o Fungi grow as molds in natural environment and in laboratory culture but yeast or yeast like structures in tissues o **Example:** Histoplasma, blastomyces and sporothrix

### Cutaneous Mycoses

Cutaneous mycoses are fungal infections usually confined to the outer layers of skin, hair, and nails, and do not invade living tissues. Also known as “ringworm” and tinea (latin “worm”) because of round shape of lesions Also called dermatophytes. There are 3 Genera: 1. Trichophyton 2. Microsporum 3. Epidermophyton **Classification: ** These can be classified on the basis of Ecology

**i.** Anthropophilic

**ii.** Zoophilic

**iii.** Geophilic

**i.** Anthropophilic: Associated with humans only. Person -to-person transmission through contaminated objects (comb, hat, etc.) All three genera i.e. Trichophyton, Microsporum, Epidermophyton

**ii.** Zoophilic: Associated with animals. Direct transmission to humans by close contact with animals. Only two genera i.e. Trichophyton and Microsporum

**iii.** Geophilic: Usually found in soil. Transmitted to humans by direct exposure Microsporum

### SYSTEMIC MYCOSES DUE TO OPPORTUNISTIC PATHOGENS

Systemic mycoses due to opportunistic pathogens are infections of patients with immune deficiencies who would otherwise not be infected. o Examples of immunocompromised conditions include AIDS, alteration of normal flora by antibiotics, immunosuppressive therapy, and metastatic cancer. o Examples of opportunistic mycoses include Candidiasis, Cryptococcosis and Aspergillosis **1.** Candidiasis **2.** Cryptococcosis **3.** Aspergillosis **1.** **Candidiasis:** Candidiasis is a primary or secondary mycotic infection caused by members of the genus Candida

**2.** **Cryptococcosis:** I nfestation with a yeast like fungus, resulting in tumors in the lungs and sometimes spreading to the brain. **Causative agents:** Cryptococcus albidus, Cryptococcus laurentii, Cryptococcus neoformans **3.** **Aspergillosis:** Aspergillosis is the name given to a wide variety of diseases caused by infection by fungi of the genus Aspergillus . The majority of cases occur in people with underlying illnesses such as tuberculosis , or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, but with otherwise healthy immune systems. **Causative agents:** Aspergillus flavus, Aspergillus fumigatus, Aspergillus nidulans, Aspergillus niger

### Treatment:

Antifungal drugs are used to treat mycoses. Depending on the nature of the infection, a topical or systemic agent may be used. Photochemotherapy or photopheresis is a technique used at medical centers for the treatment of mycosis fungoides An example of antifugal is fluconazole, which is the basis of many over-the-counter antifungal treatments. Another example is amphotericin B, which is more potent. It is used in the treatment of the most severe fungal infections that show resistance to other forms of treatment and it is administered intravenously Drugs to treat skin infections are Tolnaftate, an over the counter topical; Ketoconazole, especially used to treat tinea versicolor and other dermatophytes; Itraconazole; Terbinafine, Griseofulvin, commonly used for infections involving the scalp and nails. Yeast infections in the vagina, caused by candida albicans, can be treated with medicated suppositories whereas skin yeast infections are treated with medicated ointments

### Prevention:

Keeping the skin clean and dry, as well as maintaining good hygiene. Because fungal infections are contagious, it is important to wash after touching other people or animals with fungal disease. Sports clothing should also be washed after use.
