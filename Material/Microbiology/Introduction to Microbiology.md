**Microbiology: ** “Study of any organism too small to be seen with the human eye is called as microbiology.” Microorganisms are Oldest life form on earth. (3.5 billion years ago)

### History of Microbiology

### 1500’s -1900’s

**1500’s: ** Before the 1500’s most theories of disease were based on superstition.

No knowledge of microorganisms.

Thought that you can get disease from another person who was sick but didn’t know why.

Early Observations & Experiments: o Microscopes o van Leeuwenhoek & Hooke o Spontaneous Generation Controversy o Germ Theory of Disease & Robert Koch

**1600’s: ** Robert Hooke and Antoni van Leeuwenhoek started using crude microscopes

Van Leeuwenhoek observed what we now call bacteria and protists, he called them “animalcules”.

Called “Father of Microbiology.”

Francesco Redi 1668 Disproved Spontaneous Generation.

It was thought that organisms arose from inorganic or rotting organic material.

**1700’s ** **Edward Jenner** in 1789- Developed small pox vaccination by using a milder disease, cowpox.

He took liquid from the sores a patient with cowpox and put it into a healthy person.

Then gave that healthy person liquid from a patient with small pox and discovred that the healthy person didn’t get sick.

Risky experiment gave us the first way to prevent disease.

**1800’s More prevention ** **Ignaz Semmelweis** , hand washing before surgery could prevent “childbirth fever” i.e. Strep.

Drs. would deliver babies w/o washing hands, or after performing autopsies on women who had died from childbirth fever.

**Louis Pasteur** (1864)-demonstrated that microorganisms are present in air not created by the air- further disproving spontaneous generation.

Helped in development of germ theory of disease (microorganisms may be the cause of some or all disease).

**Joseph Lister:** Father of antiseptic surgery concept, sanitation/hygene procedure (food handlers, water).

Connected work of Semmelweis and Pasteur to develop and popularize the chemical inhibition of infection during surgery.

(washed surgical wounds with phenol carbolic acid)

**Germ Theory of Disease ** o It was proposed by **Robert Koch** in 1876.

o Bacillus antracis- caused anthrax- could take the blood of infected animals and injected blood to healthy sheep and healthy sheep got the disease.

o Koch’s postulates prove specific bacteria cause a specific disease.

o Koch’s postulates:

Microorganism must be present in every case of the disease.

The microorganism must be isolated from the diseased host and grown in pure culture.

The disease must be reproduced when the pure culture of the microorganism is inoculated into a healthy animal.

The organism must be recoverable from the experimental infected host.

**1500-1800’s ** Disease was caught from someone who was sick.

Microorganisms exist!

Disease was caused by a microorganism, that can be transferred from another person.

**Viruses:** o **Dimitri Iwanowski** (1890-1900) first to discover viruses.

o Tobacco mosaic virus

**1900’s: ** Alexander Fleming 1920’s- First antibiotics (penicillin) Fleming discovered that a mold accidentally growing on one of his petri dishes had anti-bacterial activity.

### MICROSCOPY

“Microscopy is the science of investigating small objects and structures using such an instrument.” **Types of Microscopy: ** Following are the major types of microscopy 1. Bright field microscopy 2. Dark field microscopy 3. Phase contrast microscopy and DIC microscopy 4. Fluorescent microscopy 5. Electron microscopy

**i.** Scanning electron microscopy

**ii.** Transmission electron microscopy

**1.** **Bright Field Microscopy: ** produces a dark image against a brighter background

has several objective lenses

Commonly used microscopes in labs are bright field microscopes

**2.** **Dark Field Microscopy: ** Light enters the microscope for illumination of the sample

A specially sized disc, the patch stop blocks some light from the light source, leaving an outer ring of illumination.

A wide phase annulus can also be reasonably substituted at low magnification

The condenser lens focuses the light towards the sample

The light enters the sample.

Most is directly transmitted, while some is scattered from the sample

The scattered light enters the objective lens, while the directly transmitted light simply misses the lens and is not collected due to a direct illumination block

Only the scattered light goes on to produce the image, while the directly transmitted light is omitted

**3.** **Phase Contrast Microscopy: ** Works on the principle of interference of two light rays

One is the undifracted

Other is diffracted

Let S (red) be light passing through medium surrounding sample and D (blue) light interacting with specimen.

S and D typically interfere to yield P (green), which is what we can usually detect.

P will be phase shifted compared to S, but our eyes cannot detect phase shifts.

Phase contrast microscopy effectively converts this phase shift into an intensity difference we can detect

**4.** **Florescent Microscopy: ** Works on the principle of excitation potential of the electrons of a florescent dye Energy is absorbed by the atom which becomes excited.

The electron jumps to a higher energy level.

Soon, the electron drops back to the ground state, emitting a photon (or a packet of light) the atom is fluorescing. Florescent dyes called florochromes are used for this purpose
