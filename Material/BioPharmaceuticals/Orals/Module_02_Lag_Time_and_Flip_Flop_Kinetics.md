# Module 02: Lag Time and Flip-Flop Kinetics

## Lag Time ($t_{lag}$)
In many oral formulations, there is a delay before absorption begins (e.g., due to gastric emptying or enteric coating).

### Equation with Lag Time:
$$ C = \frac{F \cdot Dose \cdot k_a}{V_d \cdot (k_a - k_e)} \cdot (e^{-k_e (t-t_{lag})} - e^{-k_a (t-t_{lag})}) $$

## Flip-Flop Kinetics
Usually, $k_a > k_e$. However, if $k_e > k_a$ (absorption is slower than elimination), the terminal slope of the semi-log plot reflects $k_a$ instead of $k_e$.

### Identification
- Common with **sustained-release** products or drugs with very poor solubility.
- To confirm, the drug must be administered IV to find the true $k_e$.
- Clinical impact: The drug stays in the body longer because it is *absorbed* slowly, not because it is *eliminated* slowly.
