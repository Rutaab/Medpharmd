# Module 10: Controlled Release and Zero-Order Absorption

## Zero-Order Absorption
In some cases, a drug is absorbed at a constant rate ($k_0$) rather than a constant fraction.
- **Examples**: Osmotic pumps (OROS), some transdermal patches.
- **Equation**:
  $$ C = \frac{k_0}{V_d \cdot k_e} \cdot (1 - e^{-k_e t}) $$

## Advantages of Controlled Release (CR)
1. **Reduced Frequency**: Better patient compliance.
2. **Stable Levels**: Less fluctuation between $C_{max}$ and $C_{min}$.
3. **Reduced Side Effects**: By avoiding high $C_{max}$.

## Potential Risks
- **Dose Dumping**: Rapid release of the entire dose due to formulation failure or alcohol interaction.
- **Large Size**: Difficulty swallowing.
- **Cost**: More expensive than immediate-release forms.
