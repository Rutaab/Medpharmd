# Module 07: First-Pass Effect and Metabolism

## Definition
The first-pass effect (or presystemic metabolism) is the phenomenon where a drug is metabolized in the liver or gut wall before it reaches the systemic circulation.

## Extraction Ratio ($ER$)
$$ ER = \frac{Cl_H}{Q_H} $$
Where $Q_H$ is hepatic blood flow (~1.5 L/min).

## Relationship to Bioavailability
If the drug is completely absorbed from the gut ($f_a = 1$) and escapes gut wall metabolism ($f_g = 1$):
$$ F = 1 - ER $$

## Clinical Implications
- Drugs with high $ER$ (e.g., Nitroglycerin, Propranolol) have very low oral bioavailability.
- High $ER$ drugs require much larger oral doses compared to IV doses.
- Liver disease can significantly increase the bioavailability of high $ER$ drugs, leading to toxicity.
