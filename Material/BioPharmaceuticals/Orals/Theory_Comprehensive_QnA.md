# Comprehensive Theory Q&A: One-Compartment Oral Administration

## Section 1: Absorption Dynamics

**Q1: How does the plasma concentration-time curve for an oral dose differ from an IV bolus?**
**A:** An IV bolus starts at $C_0$ and declines immediately. An oral dose starts at zero, rises to a peak ($C_{max}$) at time $T_{max}$ during the absorption phase, and then declines during the elimination phase.

**Q2: What is the "Absorption Rate Constant" ($k_a$)?**
**A:** $k_a$ represents the fraction of the drug dose absorbed from the GI tract into the systemic circulation per unit time. It determines the speed of absorption and the time to reach the peak.

**Q3: Define Bioavailability ($F$) and list factors that reduce it.**
**A:** Bioavailability is the fraction of an administered dose that reaches the systemic circulation in an unchanged form. It is reduced by incomplete absorption from the gut, degradation in gastric acid, and the hepatic first-pass effect.

---

## Section 2: Mathematical Parameters

**Q4: What is the significance of the "Peak Time" ($T_{max}$)?**
**A:** $T_{max}$ occurs when the rate of absorption equals the rate of elimination. It is an indicator of the absorption rate; a smaller $T_{max}$ indicates faster absorption.

**Q5: Explain "Flip-Flop Kinetics".**
**A:** Flip-flop kinetics occur when the absorption rate is much slower than the elimination rate ($k_e > k_a$). In this case, the terminal slope of the semi-log plot represents $k_a$ instead of $k_e$. This is common with extended-release formulations.

**Q6: What is the "Lag Time" ($t_{lag}$)?**
**A:** Lag time is the delay between the administration of the drug and the beginning of its absorption into the systemic circulation. It can be caused by delayed gastric emptying or the time required for a tablet to disintegrate.

---

## Section 3: Methods of Analysis

**Q7: Describe the "Method of Residuals" (Feathering).**
**A:** This is a mathematical technique used to separate the absorption and elimination phases from a biexponential oral concentration curve. By subtracting the extrapolated elimination line from the actual absorption data points, "residuals" are obtained, the slope of which gives $k_a$.

**Q8: What is the "Wagner-Nelson Method"?**
**A:** It is a model-independent method (though assuming one-compartment) used to calculate the cumulative amount of drug absorbed over time. It is useful for determining the absorption kinetics without assuming a first-order process.

---

## Section 4: Clinical and Physiological Factors

**Q9: How does the First-Pass Effect impact dosing?**
**A:** Drugs with high first-pass metabolism (high extraction ratio) require much larger oral doses than IV doses to achieve the same therapeutic effect because a large portion of the oral dose is destroyed by the liver before reaching the heart.

**Q10: Why is the small intestine the primary site for drug absorption?**
**A:** Due to its massive surface area provided by villi and microvilli, and its high blood flow. Most drugs, whether acidic or basic, are absorbed more rapidly in the small intestine than in the stomach.
