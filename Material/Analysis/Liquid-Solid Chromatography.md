# Liquid-Solid Chromatography (LSC)
Liquid-Solid Chromatography (LSC) is a type of liquid chromatography where the stationary phase is a solid and the mobile phase is a liquid.
## Principle and Mechanism
The separation mechanism in LSC is based on the competition of the components of the mixture sample for the active sites on an adsorbent.
This is an adsorption chromatography process, involving distribution between two phases through adsorption and desorption.
Attractive forces such as ionic, dipole-dipole, and dipole-induced dipole interactions play a role.
Good adsorbents are characterized by a large surface area and more active sites.
Equilibration occurs at the surface-solute, surface-solvent, and solvent-solute interfaces.
## Adsorption Isotherms
An adsorption isotherm plots the concentration or amount of analyte on the adsorbent surface as a function of its concentration in the bulk phase (mobile phase). Types include:
Linear: where the partition coefficient (k = Cs / Cm) equals 1.
## Adsorbents
The adsorbing power of a material depends on its chemical nature, available surface area, and pretreatment. Commonly used adsorbents include:
Alumina (Al2O3): Can be acidic, basic, or neutral, and is available in various grades.
Silica gel (silicon dioxide): Acidic in nature and available in various grades; it has a high capacity and is useful for both adsorption and partition chromatography.

Sucrose.
Starch.
Cellulose. Adsorbents should have uniform size and a large surface area. The weight of the adsorbent should typically be 20-50 times more than the sample.
## Solvents & Adsorbates
Since adsorbents are generally polar, non-polar compounds elute first.
The typical elution order for adsorbates, from non-polar to polar, is: alkyl halides < saturated hydrocarbons < unsaturated hydrocarbons < ethers < esters < ketones < amines < alcohols < phenols < acids and bases.
Polymeric compounds and salts often do not elute.
Solvents are ordered by increasing polarity (elution strength): Hexane/Petroleum ether < CCl4 < toluene < dichloromethane < chloroform < diethyl ether < acetone < ethyl acetate < propanol < ethanol < methanol < acetic acid < water.
## Columns and Packing
Various sizes of columns are available, and packing can be done using either wet method or dry method.
## Example Application
LSC is used for separating water-soluble vitamins.
