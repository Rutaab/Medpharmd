# Molecular Fluorescence Spectroscopy
Molecular Fluorescence Spectroscopy (FS) is an analytical technique that measures the concentration of a fluorescent substance in a sample by utilizing the phenomenon of fluorescence, which is the emission of light by a substance after it absorbs light or electromagnetic radiation.
## Principle
This technique is based on the emission of light by molecules that have become electronically excited after absorbing electromagnetic radiation. It typically uses UV-visible spectra along with some part of the IR region of the electromagnetic spectrum. It is a highly sensitive technique, capable of detecting samples in the parts per million (ppm) to parts per trillion (ppt) range.
Jablonski Diagram: The process is explained by the Jablonski Diagram: an electron in its ground state (S₀) absorbs light (EMR) and gets excited to a high intermediate energy level. According to Kasha's rule, it moves to a stable energy level (S₁). From this stable excited state, the electron then returns to its ground state (S₀) either non-radiatively (losing energy as heat during interconversion) or radiatively, producing fluorescence.
## Properties of Fluorescence
The emitted light usually has a longer wavelength (lower energy)** than the absorbed exciting light, because some energy is lost as heat.
The emitted light is composed of many wavelengths, forming a fluorescence spectrum.
Fluorescence is a specific property of each molecule and depends on its auxochrome.
## Types of Fluorescence
Prompt Fluorescence: Immediate release of electromagnetic energy from the singlet state (S₁ → S₀ + hν).
Delayed Fluorescence: Results from two intersystem crossings (S₀ → S₁ → T₁ → S₁ → S₀ + hν). This can be E-type (thermal energy interconversion) or P-type (collision energy interconversion).
Phospholuminescence: A delayed release of electromagnetic energy from the triplet state (S₀ → S₁ → T₁ → S₀ + hν). Its intensity is lower as the electron loses energy during intersystem crossing.
## Instrumentation
A typical fluorescence spectrometer includes:
Light Source: Commonly mercury-arc lamps or xenon-arc lamps, which provide a steady and homogeneous supply of light to energize electrons from the ground state to an excited state.
Excitation Monochromator: Used to select the specific wavelength of light to fall on the sample, ensuring that the sample is excited at the desired energy.
Sample Holder: A cuvette or quartz cell, which must be transparent from all sides to allow the produced fluorescence to be observed by the detector.
Emission Monochromator: A second monochromator is placed at a right angle to the excitation monochromator. It filters and directs the emitted fluorescence towards the detector, allowing only the desired wavelengths to be observed.
Detector: Observes the produced fluorescence and generates an electrical signal, which is then amplified. Common detectors include phototubes and photomultiplier tubes.
Read Out Devices: A computer reads the detected signal and plots a graph (spectrum).
## Applications
While not all substances fluoresce, FS has several important applications:
Qualitative and Quantitative Analysis: Used for both identifying and quantifying fluorescent substances.
Protein Identification: Achieved by tagging a fluorescent molecule within the protein and observing its fluorescence.
Assay of Medications: Used for drugs that exhibit fluorescence (e.g., Amphotericin B, Diphenhydramine, Flurazepam, Reserpine).
Quality Control of Crude Drugs: Powdered crude drugs can be analyzed for fluorescent properties under visible and UV light for quality control.
Quantitative Analysis of Specific Compounds: Such as riboflavin, chloroquine, quinine, adrenaline, reserpine, phenobarbitone, and retinol.
Derivatization: For compounds that are not inherently fluorescent, derivatization methods (direct labeling or changing solvent/medium) can be employed to make them fluorescent.
Quenching and Replacement Techniques: Used when derivatization is not feasible to analyze fluorescent inactive compounds or separate multiple solutes.
